Sky conditions in the wind chart — three treatments, same 48 h, 3-hour granularity (cloud cover %, rain mm/h). Illustrative sky data.
A · Sky band (recommended)
A 14 pt strip above the plot, one cell per 3 h: clear = faint sun-amber, partly = light grey, overcast = darker grey; rain = 1–3 short blue ticks by intensity. Rect + line marks only; no icons, no text, no legend needed after the first look. Shares the x-scale, so you read sky and wind in one glance.
At arm's length on a dock it still reads: dark = cloud, blue = rain. It never competes with the bars because it has no height. Costs 16 pt of vertical space.
B · Glyph row
SF Symbols every 6 h (sun, cloud.sun, cloud, cloud.rain). The thing every weather app does.
It breaks the brief's "no sun/cloud icons" rule, and at 6 h one glyph stands for a lot of sky. It reads as weather first, wind second — the eye goes to pictures before bars. Shown so the comparison is fair, not because I'd ship it.

Nothing on the chart. Conditions appear as text in the readout while scrubbing (and in the web tooltip): "Overcast · rain 2 mm/h · 48°F". The 48 h chart stays exactly as it is.
Zero cost to the one job; large cost to glanceability — you only learn it's going to rain by touching the hour. Good as the detail layer under A, weak alone.
