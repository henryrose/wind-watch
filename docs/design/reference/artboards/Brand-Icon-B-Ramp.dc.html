B·1 · Grown from the scale
A five-step strip (B2–B6) along the bottom edge. The B4 step is the same green as the tile, so the ground reads as the B4 step grown up to fill the icon — the green finally has a reason. Arrow silhouette untouched, so tinted mode is still the plain B. Strip is 28/180 tall: about 4 pt at 29 pt, still visible as colour.
tinted
B·2 · Stepped arrow
The arrow itself carries the ramp in five hard bands, calm at the tail to B6 at the tip — wind building as it travels. Dark ground so every band has contrast. Most rainbow of the three and the most memorable; also the one that drifts furthest from "all business", and at 29 pt it reads more as a colourful arrow than as Beaufort.
tinted
B·3 · Wake
White arrow on dark, with three short streaks trailing behind it in B2/B3/B4 — a little wind wake. Most "weather-y" and the softest colour dose. The streaks are a new mark the chart never uses, which breaks the rule that the brand only reuses chart shapes, and they turn to mush below 40 pt.
Decided: B·3 "Wake" — small-size problem solved with size tiers on "Brand · icon final". Original note: I had recommended B·1. It adds the ramp without touching the mark: the arrow on green still does all the recognition work, the strip is a signature you notice the second time. It also stays honest to the system — the colours appear in their real order and stepped, exactly as on the bars. iOS 26 layered build: background layer = green + strip, foreground glyph layer = arrow; in tinted/clear modes the strip collapses to luminance steps and the arrow silhouette survives.
Lockups follow the icon: the 18 pt tile in the iPhone eyebrow and the 28 pt web tile both carry the strip (it's ~3 pt at 18 — keep it). Below 16 pt (favicon 16, watch settings footer) drop the strip and use plain B. Amber/red/purple stay out: the strip stops at B6 orange so the icon never looks like a warning.
